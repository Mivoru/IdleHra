# Roadmap after the War tree - plans and proposals

Written 2026-10-30, after the War skill tree went live for every player
(#225-#229, server 1.0.1356, client 1.39.1). The owner asked for detailed
plans for the most important open work, and for the next phases of the
rework: 2b, affixes, Hearth, and affix families with signature affixes.

Each plan has the same shape: the goal, what is true today (checked in the
code on 2026-10-30), the steps, what to measure, the acceptance, the risks
and a size. **S** is one small PR, **M** is one PR of a day's work, **L** is
2-4 PRs, and **XL** is a phase with its own sub-plan. Owner decisions are
marked **DECIDE**. Each of them comes with a recommendation.

## 2. Bugs and test reliability

### 2.1 `exercise.mjs` flakes (#120, issue #204) - M, top priority

**Goal.** A green run means the game works, and a red run means it does
not. Nobody reruns to find out which.

**Today.** On 2026-10-29 three different single steps failed on working
builds:
- the Delve step ("the gold actually left"): an offline payout landed
  mid-step;
- "a mature account is told what to do next": spent fixture state;
- "a drop can be worn from the loot list": a fixed 90 s wait on drop RNG.

On 2026-10-30 two full runs passed 388/388, so the flakes are intermittent,
not gone.

**Steps.**
1. **Measure first.** Run `exercise` 5 times on an unchanged build and record
   every failing step with its message. If it is green 5/5, record that and
   go on to step 2 anyway: the three causes are real.
2. **Seed at the start.** `exercise` calls the idempotent `--seed-dev` (or a
   dev REST endpoint that does the same) before it signs in. The seed stamps
   the fixture's last-seen time to now, so no offline payout can land
   mid-run.
3. **The Delve step** compares gold against the debit it caused (read the
   Delve's own ledger row from `gold_spend_daily`), not against the wallet,
   which offline income can move.
4. **The wearable-drop step** gets its drop from a dev grant (the pet-drop
   step's `POST /api/v1/dev/...` pattern) instead of waiting on RNG.
5. **The "told what to do next" step** restores what it spends, as the
   CLAUDE.md rule requires ("a check that spends fixture state passes once
   and fails forever").
6. **`EXERCISE_ONLY=<sections>`** runs only the named sections. A full run
   takes about 25 minutes, and most changes touch one area.

**Acceptance.** 5 consecutive full runs green on an unchanged build, with
their counts recorded in the PR. Each fixed step still passes when re-run
after the fixture is deliberately left spent.

**Risk.** A dev grant endpoint must stay dev-only. Copy the guard the
pet-drop endpoint uses.

### 2.2 Flaky server tests - S-M

**Today.** Two tests fail only under full-suite load, and each passes alone:
- `OfflineCombatParityTests` row "r5 bare fed" (TASK_BOARD);
- `AutomationAutoFuseTests.TheWorker_FusesTheStacksARequestsDropsLandedIn`
  (seen every full local run on 2026-10-30).

Both touch process-wide static state: `CombatLootEngine.DropRequestQueue`
and `GatheringGrantQueue` are `static readonly ConcurrentQueue`s, and xUnit
runs test classes in parallel. A test that drains a queue can take another
test's items, or leave its own for someone else.

**Steps.**
1. List every test that enqueues to or drains these queues (grep
   `DropRequestQueue`, `GatheringGrantQueue`).
2. Put them in one xUnit collection with parallelisation off for that
   collection (`[Collection("Static loot queues")]` with
   `DisableParallelization = true`), the cheapest fix. Or have each test
   drain only its own player id and leave the rest in the queue.
3. Do not widen any tolerance until the shared state is ruled out.

**Acceptance.** 5 full local suite runs green. CI was already green, because
it runs fewer tests in parallel.

## 5. Phase 2b - one combat model (the per-swing simulator)

### 5.1 Why

Live combat rolls every swing (`SimulationEngine.RunCombatTick`). Offline
catch-up, the hunting advisor and warp use an expected-value model
(`HuntingProjection`, `OfflineSimulationEngine.ProjectCombatLegs`). The
two are held together by parity tests (`OfflineCombatParityTests`,
`WarMechanicParityTests`). It works, but:
- every mechanic is built twice: a live roll and a closed-form
  expectation. That is the "wired on one side only" defect class, which
  the parity tests catch only after the fact;
- mechanics that carry state between swings cannot exist: Bleed (damage
  over time), Momentum (stacks), Sunder (a debuff on the monster), low-HP
  bonuses, and the original Counterstroke ("after a dodge, the next swing
  crits"). Rule 4.1 of the design bans them for exactly this reason.

**The goal: offline combat runs the same resolution code as the live
tick**, swing by swing, with a seeded RNG. One model, not two.

### 5.2 Design

- **Extract the resolution, do not copy it.** Split the per-swing work in
  `RunCombatTick` (about 1,500 lines today) into two pure functions on a
  `CombatState` struct: `ResolvePlayerSwing(ref CombatState, in
  CombatContext, IRng)` and `ResolveMonsterSwing(...)`. The state holds
  player and monster health, the swing accumulators, the War counters,
  `LastHitWasCrit`, the eat cooldown, food, rations, Death Ward and the
  Last Stand timer. The context holds the stats, the monster, the codex
  and the tree effects. The live tick calls them inside its tick timing;
  the simulator jumps from swing to swing.
- **RNG.** An `IRng` interface. Live keeps `Random.Shared`. The simulator
  uses a seeded `Random` (player id x offline-window start), so an offline
  payout can be replayed in a test.
- **Events stay out of the simulator.** Kills, XP, gold, loot requests,
  deaths and food come back as counts and totals, not as packets. The live
  tick keeps its feed (`ResponseCombatEventPacket`).
- **The advisor stays on expectations at first.** `HuntingProjection`
  answers instantly for every monster on the Combat screen. A later step
  may replace it with a short Monte Carlo run of the simulator.

### 5.3 Steps (one PR each)

1. **2b.1 Extract.** Move per-swing resolution out of `RunCombatTick` into
   the two functions, and keep the live tick calling them. **No behaviour
   change.** Proof: every parity test and `exercise` unchanged, plus a new
   test that runs a seeded live hour twice and gets identical results.
2. **2b.2 The simulator.** `CombatSimulator.Run(payload, seconds, seed)`,
   which loops over the two functions plus the between-swing rules
   (respawn, auto-eat, rations, regen). Measure it against the live tick
   over many seeded hours. Kills, XP, gold, food and deaths must agree
   within sampling error, which is exact in distribution, not in mean.
   Also check it against `ProjectCombatLegs` to see what changes.
3. **2b.3 Performance.** Benchmark 12 offline hours for a geared region-5
   character and a cold-recovery batch of every account. Budget: 12 h under
   50 ms per account (design estimate: about 29,000 player swings), and
   cold recovery not measurably slower.
4. **2b.4 The switch, behind a flag.** Offline catch-up uses the simulator
   when `FOLKIDLE_OFFLINE_SIM=1`. Deploy with the flag off, turn it on, and
   compare production's offline/online gold-per-hour ratio from
   `gold_income_daily` (`CombatAway` against `Combat`) for a week. The
   ratio must not move by more than 3%.
5. **2b.5 Retire the duplicate.** Remove `ProjectCombatLegs`' combat half.
   The parity tests become simulator-against-live tests. `HuntingProjection`
   stays for the advisor.
6. **2b.6 Stateful mechanics (design pass with the owner).** Candidates for
   the War tree or signature affixes: Bleed (a share of each crit as damage
   over 3 s), Momentum (+1% damage per consecutive hit, reset on a miss),
   Sunder (each hit takes 1% armour off the monster, up to 10 stacks), the
   original Counterstroke, and "below 30% health, +X% damage".

### 5.4 Risks

- **Offline payout drift**, the defect class this codebase has shipped
  before. The flag, the week of production comparison and step 2b.1's
  no-behaviour-change proof are the guard.
- **Cold recovery time:** every session is reconstructed at boot. Step
  2b.3 measures it before the switch.
- **The size of `RunCombatTick`:** step 2b.1 is the hard part. Keep it
  mechanical, and keep the live tick's order of operations.

**Size: XL, about 6 PRs.**

## 6. Phase 3 - the affix rework

Design: `SKILL_TREE_REWORK.md` section 10. Updated here for what changed
since it was written.

### 6.1 Corrections to the design

- **`crushing_blow_pct`** now adds to Crushing Blow's chance to **double**
  a hit. The design says "ignores armour", which was the version dropped
  in slice 2.3. The live roll and `Landed` already read
  `WarEffects.CrushingChance`; the affix adds to it.
- **`double_hit_pct`** is "a swing repeats in full, no recursion". It is a
  different thing from Double Strike (a crit lands twice) and from Echo (a
  crit repeats at half), so it needs its own roll and its own expectation.
- **`boss_dmg_pct` moves walls.** Bossbreaker lost its boss damage for this
  reason. `BossGearBenchmark` builds its reference gear from the roll
  pools, so a boss-damage affix in the pools raises the benchmark
  character's damage on every wall. Two options:
  - **(a)** Keep it, and re-solve the walls with `WallWindowScanTests`.
  - **(b)** Make it world-boss damage only (`WorldBossDamageTenthsPct`
    exists, and pets already grant it).
  **DECIDE. Recommendation: (b).** It keeps the walls, and gives the world
  boss leaderboard a gear stat.

### 6.2 Steps

1. **3.1 The damage family (S).** New rolls use `melee_dmg_pct` only;
   `range_dmg_pct` and `magic_dmg_pct` leave the pools, and existing items
   keep them. The weapon pool goes from 8 to 6 types. Measure the
   benchmark gear's DPS before and after (it should rise a little, since
   fewer rolls are spent on duplicate damage) and run the wall scan.
   **Built 2026-10-30 (`AffixDefinition.Retired`).** Measured: it FELL a
   little. A five-affix weapon takes five distinct types, so with three
   damage ids it could carry damage three times; now once. The wall scan's
   kill times rose 2-7% (region 2 T8/T8 389 s -> 410 s, region 3 T8/T7
   743 s -> 798 s) and no WIN/die cell flipped, so the walls hold.
2. **3.2 New affixes (M-L).** `crushing_blow_pct`, `double_hit_pct`,
   `boss_dmg_pct` (or world-boss only), `loot_luck_pct`, `gold_find_pct`
   and `thorns_pct`, appended to `AffixRegistry.Definitions` (never
   inserted: the order is a wire index). For each one:
   - a consumer;
   - a parity row if it touches combat;
   - its `StatAuditTests` probe, which runs over every affix id
     automatically;
   - the client's `KNOWN_AFFIX_IDS` (`serverMirrors.test.ts` compares
     them);
   - its tooltip.
   **Partly built 2026-10-30:** `boss_dmg_pct` (world boss only), `loot_luck_pct`
   and `gold_find_pct`, flagged `Utility` (BossGearBenchmark skips them, so no
   wall moves). Sizes: 3-11%, 1.5-5.5% and 2-8% by affix rarity, under the
   pet (8-10%) and War nodes (Scavenger 7.5%, Golden Touch 10%) that pay the
   same stats. Still open: `crushing_blow_pct`, `double_hit_pct`, `thorns_pct`
   (they need combat code and parity rows).
3. **3.3 The ring and amulet pools (S), D4.** **Utility half built** (ring:
   block, world boss, loot luck, gold find; amulet adds loot luck and gold
   find). **Combat half deferred to 3.5:** crit chance on the ring and crit
   damage on the amulet moved every wall and tier 6 when measured, because the
   benchmark ring stacked block five times (about 21% at the top). The ring rolls block, crit
   chance, double hit, boss (or world boss), loot luck and gold find. The
   amulet adds crit damage, loot luck and gold find. Stored items keep
   their affixes.
4. **3.4 Refine (M).** Keep an affix's type and rarity, and reroll only
   its value within ±20%, for a fraction of a Recast's price. A new
   command (the `add-command` skill), a button on the item, and a gold
   ledger category. **DECIDE:** the price. Proposal: 25% of the region's

   stop conditions (rarity, stat) can never be met by a Refine. Price
   250-2,500 gold by region (a quarter of 1,000-10,000), ledger category
   `Refine`. exercise refines on the fixture and puts payload and gold back

5. **3.5 Re-measure.** Run the tree harness (the gear moved), the wall

## 7. Phase 4 - the Hearth half

Design: `SKILL_TREE_REWORK.md` section 5. Hearth is a second tree, paid
with **craft points** earned from profession mastery. It never costs a
combat point, and combat never costs it one.

### 7.1 Step 4.0 - measure first (D2) - S

D2 waits on this.
- **The mastery spread (read-only production query):** Woodcutting,
  Mining, Fishing and Herbalism mastery levels per player (the
  `TickStatePayload` `*MasteryLevel` fields come from their table), plus
  Cooking and Alchemy (`PlayerProductionRegistry`). Show the distribution
  by player level.
- **Mastery per hour** at reference builds, from `ProgressionRateTests`.
- **Rebirth:** does mastery survive a rebirth today? If it does, Hearth
  should too (the design's D2 note).

**From the numbers, propose the craft-point rate.** The aim is that a
player who gathers a fifth of their time (the `GatheringShareTests`
target) earns Hearth points at about the pace War points come, roughly
one per account level. For example: one craft point per N summed mastery
levels, with N chosen so that the median level-60 player holds about 30
points.

#### Measured 2026-10-30 (read-only production query)

| Player | Level (peak) | Rebirths | Wood | Mine | Fish | Herb | Cook | Alch | Sum |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mivoru | 111 (112) | 1 | 367 | 375 | 190 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 932 |
| Tomda | 98 | 0 | 349 | 288 | 256 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 893 |
| (pro)boss | 99 | 0 | 250 | 235 | 316 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 801 |

No other account is past level 5, so there is no level-60 player to take a
median from. What the numbers and the code say:
- **Mastery survives a rebirth.** `SeasonalRotationEngine.ResetPlayersAsync`
  resets level, XP, attributes, skill points, potions and the life region,
  and no mastery column. Mivoru (one rebirth) kept 932. So, per D2, **Hearth
  survives a rebirth too**.
- **Mastery has no cap.** `MasteryXpForLevel` is `50 x (level + 1)^2`, so a
  level costs more each time and summed mastery grows roughly with the cube
  root of the hours spent.
- **Cooking and Alchemy mastery have no writer.** Nothing in the server
  advances `CookingMasteryLevel` or `AlchemyMasteryLevel`, so they are 0 for
  everyone. **Herbalism** has a writer (`AdvanceMastery` for profession 3) and
  is still 0 for everyone. Leave all three out of the rate until they can
  move, or the rate counts a number nobody can raise.
- The three active accounts each run two gatherers all the time (task 111's
  query), so their sums are close to the ceiling of what gathering pays
  today.

**Proposed rate: 1 craft point per 10 summed levels of Woodcutting, Mining
and Fishing (Herbalism added when it can move), derived, never stored.**
Today that is 93, 89 and 80 points for accounts at levels 98-111, about one
point per account level: the War tree's pace, which is what section 7.1
asked for. Because mastery grows more slowly over time, the pace slows down
for a player who keeps gathering, never speeds up. The Hearth tree's full cost
should be set so that about 90 points buy a strong build and not all of it,
as the War tree is today.

**DECIDED (D2), owner 2026-10-30:** the rate above, and Hearth survives a
rebirth. The per-hour numbers from `ProgressionRateTests` still have to be
run to check the early game (how many points at levels 20 and 40), before
step 4.2 fixes the formula.

### 7.2 The rank buffer has no room - DECIDE (step 4.1, M)

`SkillRankBuffer.Length` is 64. Ids 0-19 are the old tree's slots,
emptied by the refund but still read by the Craft, Harvest and Scholar
hooks. 20-54 are War. Only 55-63 are free, which is 9 slots. Hearth needs
about 30. Options:
- **(a)** Grow the buffer to 128. The payload is blitted (`MemoryMarshal`
  in the state dump), so check every place that serialises
  `TickStatePayload`. A packet does not carry it: the ranks left the
  snapshot in #217.
- **(b)** Reuse ids 0-19 for Hearth. That is only safe after
  `ReconcileWarSwitchAsync` stops deleting every row below 20. It would

## 8. Phase 5 - affix families and signature affixes

### 8.1 What these are

- **Affix families.** Each affix belongs to one family that matches a tree
  column: Power (Might), Precision, Guard, Fortune, and Craft (Hearth).
  One node per column says "+X% to the values of your <family> affixes".
  A Might player then wants Power affixes on their gear, and the tree
  tells them what to look for when they reroll. **The link between tree
  and gear.** It is a relative boost of up to +10%, and the tooltip says
  so ("your crit chance affixes are 10% stronger").
- **Signature affixes.** Ordinary affixes are numbers ("+3% crit
  chance"). A signature affix is a **mechanic** on an item, like a small
  keystone. Examples:
  - "Your Crushing Blows heal 1% of your health"
  - "Echo can follow a Riposte too"
  - "Every 50th kill drops a material of the next region"
  - "Ruin's every 8th hit is also a crit"

  The tree supplies the mechanics, and the gear amplifies them. A
  signature should be **rare**: one slot, only on Legendary items or
  better, or only through fusion. It is what a player hunts for at the
  top of the game.

### 8.2 Plan

1. **5.1 Families (M).** A family tag on every `AffixDefinition`, one
   multiplier per family in `EquipmentSlotEngine.AddAffixTotals`, one node
   per column (War and Hearth) at +3/6/10%, and a tooltip that shows the
   boosted value. Measure through the tree harness, because it raises gear
   power: the ceiling must stay at 0.95-1.05x.
2. **5.2 Signatures, the design pass (S, with the owner).** Pick about 8
   signatures that each pass rule 4.1, or the simulator after 2b. Each one
   names the tree mechanic it amplifies.
3. **5.3 Signatures, the build (L).** A signature slot on the item
   (persisted, so it needs a migration), a roll rule (Legendary or better,
   or fusion), the mechanic in the live tick and in offline, a callout,
   and a parity row each.

**Size: L-XL.** It waits on phase 3, and is best after 2b, because most
signatures worth having carry state.
