# Audit, 2026-09-05

Run after tasks 1-10 closed, looking for the defect shapes this codebase
actually produces rather than for style. Everything below was checked
mechanically or in the source; nothing here is a hunch.

The scans are described precisely enough to rebuild, because they are worth
re-running and none of them is worth keeping as a script nobody runs.

---

## What was checked

| Scan | Question | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Wire hydration | Which fields reach the client but are never loaded at login? | **1 real defect, fixed** |

## Fixed in this pass

### 1. World boss attempts did not survive a logout — SILENT

`WorldBossAttemptCount` was written in exactly one place, the notification
raised after an attack resolves, and read straight onto the wire. **Nothing
loaded it.** A player who spent their attempts, logged out and came back saw
three unspent pips; clicking Attack hit the cap inside `ExecuteAttackAsync`,
which rolls back with no damage, no message and no telemetry they would ever
see.

Found by the exercise script's own numbers, not by reading: it reported an
attempt going **"0 -> 2 spent" on a single strike**, which is not a thing one
strike can do.

### 2. The battle session cap was invisible from every angle — WORSE

`WorldBossEngine.BattleSessionCapSeconds` is **300**. A player has five minutes
from their FIRST strike to spend the other two — inside an encounter that runs
for **up to seven days**. After that every attack rolls back in silence and the
button stays enabled forever.

**An idle player who strikes once and comes back an hour later is the normal
case in this genre.** It silently cost them two thirds of their participation.
The screen's own header comment already listed this as one of three silent
rollbacks it existed to explain — and it explained the other two.

`WorldBossSessionEndsEpoch` is on the wire now, hydrated at login and refreshed
on every attack; the screen counts down while the session is open and says so
when it closes. **The 300-second value itself is untouched** — see the proposals.

### 3. Six newly hand-mirrored constants had no guard

The world boss rework added `BOSS_PLATE_COUNT`, `BOSS_WEAK_PLATE_MULTIPLIER`,
`BOSS_WEAK_PLATE_HIDDEN` and `BOSS_SESSION_CAP_SECONDS` to the client, and
inherited `MAX_BOSS_ATTEMPTS`, which had never been guarded at all. The combat
log added six event kinds and four flags decoded from raw numbers.

A mismatch in the log constants would not throw — it would silently **relabel
every line**, which is worse than a crash: "You miss" where the server said
"Critical". All of them are in `serverMirrors.test.ts` now.

### 4. A test that would have passed four runs in five

`Test_WorldBoss_AttemptLimitingAndScaling` asserts damage lands exactly as sent.
A randomly-seeded weak plate takes triple, so a hardcoded plate 0 would have
failed one run in five. A test that passes four times out of five is worse than
one that fails, because the failure stops being information.

## Found and NOT fixed — proposals

### A. The affix lock was completely inert. **BUILT.**

`EquipmentInstance.IsAffixLocked` is:

- **read** in ten places — `ForgeSplicingEngine` (which has a whole
  `FailedAffixLocked` result code), `AffixRerollEngine`,
  `ClientCommandValidator`, `MarketEscrowEngine`, two REST projections
- **written** in three places, always to `false`
- **never set to `true` anywhere**, and no command exists to set it
- also readable from the affix payload JSON as `"is_affix_locked":true`, which
  nothing writes either

The client has the full apparatus too: three wire bytes
(`Equipped{Weapon,Armor,Leggings}AffixLocked`), a `lockField` on three slot
descriptors, and an `isAffixLocked()` helper **that no screen calls**.

So a player can never lock an affix, `FailedAffixLocked` can never occur, and
three bytes ride every snapshot at 10 Hz carrying a constant zero.

This is the inverse of this project's usual defect — the READ side is
thoroughly wired and the write side does not exist — and it is just as much a
half-built system that looks finished.

**It is also a good feature, and the chest sweep is what makes it urgent.**
`RemoveEquipmentUpToTierAsync` deletes every unworn piece at or below a quality
tier in one statement. Its rarity ceiling of 6 is the ONLY thing standing
between a player and a favourite Epic sword - there is no way to say "not that
one".

**Half of this was fixed rather than proposed.** The lock meant less than its
name: it was honoured by the reroll and by forge fusion and IGNORED by both
removal paths, so a locked item could not be changed but could still be sold,
binned or swept. Both paths respect it now, with a `ChestActionResult.Locked`
the client can name, and `ChestLockTests` pins it by setting the flag directly -
the only way to prove the behaviour is in place BEFORE the feature that needs it
exists, rather than being remembered on the day.

Writing that found a second trap immediately: the sweep's DELETE repeats the
SELECT's predicate rather than listing the ids it just read, which is correct
inside one Serializable transaction and wrong the moment the two differ by a
term. Adding the lock to the SELECT alone would have deleted locked pieces
while paying for none of them.

**And the write side shipped too, so finding A is now closed.**
`VillageChestEngine.ToggleAffixLockAsync` behind `POST /api/v1/chest/lock`, with
a Lock button on every equipment row in the chest. Free, deliberately: this is a
safety flag on an item the player already owns, and charging for "do not delete
my sword" would be a strange thing to sell.

Three details worth keeping:

- **It toggles rather than sets.** The caller asks for a flip and is told the
  state it landed in, so two clicks racing cannot leave the screen showing
  whichever one lost.
- **Ownership is decided in the SELECT**, scoped to the player, and somebody
  else's item reads as `NotFound` rather than as a refusal that would confirm it
  exists.
- **It is REST, not a new opcode.** The chest is entirely REST already - sell,
  bin, sweep, settings - and a new WS command would have been the only one of
  its kind. A first attempt did add opcode 76 before that was noticed; adding a
  dead opcode while auditing for dead opcodes would have been a poor joke.

### B. `RegisterWorldBossDamage` (opcode 19) is declared but retired

The handler was deliberately removed — it was a second entry into
`WorldBossEngine.QueueAttack` with weaker validation, taking the damage figure
straight out of `cmd.TargetId`. The comment recording that is still in
`SimulationEngine`, but **the enum value is still declared**, so it is still on
the generated client protocol and still accepted by the wire (it falls through
the dispatch chain and does nothing).

**Recommended: delete the enum value.** It costs nothing to remove and it stops
a future reader from wiring it back up.

### C. Four orphan logs in `items.json`

`glacier_pine_log`, `ironwood_log`, `void_bark_log`, `whispering_willow_log`
exist in `items.json` and **nowhere else**: no server code references them, no
gathering node pays them, no recipe consumes them, and they are not in the ORE
canon. A player can never obtain them and they do nothing.

They do reach the client's generated item-name and sprite maps, so they cost
sprite budget and can surface in the Wiki.

**Recommended: decide deliberately.** Either give them a gathering node — the
names suggest a sixth region's woods — or remove them. Leaving unobtainable
items in the catalogue is how `docs/crafting_material_audit.md` started.

### D. The material-namespace rule holds, and its examples are worth pinning

Checked because CLAUDE.md calls it out as having caused six defects in one day.
**The rule is intact and CLAUDE.md's own examples are correct**: `raw_log` and
`wood` genuinely have no `items.json` entry, and so do `raw_fish`, `herb` and
`stone`.

What is worth recording is the *other* half, because it changed and nothing says
so: **`copper_ore`, `iron_ore`, `obsidian_ore` and `silver_ore` DO have entries
now.** The comment in `VillageManagementEngine.TierMaterials` records them being
added — the mining loot table had been pointing at `*_crafting_material`
variants precisely because those four had nothing to point at. All twenty canon
ore and log slugs now have a `BaseId`.

So the trap has moved: it is no longer "ores are commodity slugs" but "*some*
material slugs are catalogued and some are not, and the gathering primitives
are the ones that are not". Anyone reasoning from "ore = no ItemDefinition"
would now be wrong in the opposite direction.

**And it closes a defect that was recorded as open.** All twenty
`GuildContributionEngine.BuffTierMaterials` slugs now resolve through
`TryGetItemDefinitionByBaseId`, so every buff tier's common-ore path works.
The known issue was "four of the twenty buff materials are undonatable because
the four common ores have no `ItemDefinition`" — that is no longer true, and
nothing said so. Verified by resolving all twenty against `items.json`.

**No change needed in the repo.** Recorded here because it is the kind of fact
that is only ever discovered by tripping over it.

### E. The 300-second battle session cap fights the genre

Now that it is visible, it is worth asking whether it should exist.

Three attempts, and five minutes to use them, inside a seven-day window. The
player who is punished is the one who opens the game, strikes once, and closes
it — which is **the defining play pattern of an idle game**. The player who is
rewarded is the one who sits on the screen.

That is backwards for this genre, and it is the same shape as two things this
project has already reverted: the anti-cheat that banned fast clickers, and the
four active skills measured at "+90% damage for clicking every three seconds".

**Recommended: raise it to the length of the event window, or delete it.** If it
exists to stop something, that something should be written down first — nothing
in the source says what it is for.

### F. Two dead fields on `TickStatePayload`

`WorldBossMaxHp` and `WorldBossCurrentHp` are never read or written: the
broadcast populates the packet from `_worldBossEngine` directly. Harmless, 16
bytes of per-player state that says something untrue about where the value comes
from. **Recommended: delete.**

### G. Two misleading signatures

- `StatsCalculator.Calculate(..., int humanMastery = 0, ...)` never reads
  `humanMastery`. Human mastery grants XP and vault slots, neither of which is a
  combat stat, so this is correct behaviour with a misleading parameter.
- `CraftingEngine.GetMaxForgeTierForRegion(int regionTier)` returns
  `ForgeSplicingEngine.MaxQualityTier` and ignores the region. That is
  deliberate — a test pins it for regions 1, 2, 3 and 9 — but the name promises
  a per-region answer it does not give.

**Recommended: drop the parameter in the first case, rename in the second.**
Both are the kind of small dishonesty that costs someone an hour later.

## What the scans could become

The **wire hydration scan** is the one worth keeping. It found a real,
player-visible, silent defect, and the invariant it checks is genuine: *a field
that travels to the client is either loaded at login or is deliberately
per-session state*.

It cannot be a test without a whitelist, because 36 fields are legitimately
runtime-only (combat state, the offline summary, the victory and death cards).
A whitelist is maintenance — but it is the kind that fails loudly and correctly
when someone adds a field and forgets to load it, which is exactly what
happened here.

**Recommended: build it as a server test with an explicit
`RuntimeOnlyByDesign` list**, so adding a wire field forces a decision about
where its value comes from after a relogin.
